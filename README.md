# Violent Crime as a Threat to Societal Security: The Case of Romania

**Original title:** *Criminalitatea violentă ca amenințare la securitatea societală*

Bachelor's thesis in Security Studies, Faculty of History and Philosophy, Babeș-Bolyai University, Cluj-Napoca (2026).

The thesis is written in Romanian. This README summarizes it in English.

## Overview

Security studies have moved beyond purely military threats to include non-military ones that affect how societies function. This thesis asks whether violent crime is one of them, and if so, how it undermines social stability. It combines criminology with the Copenhagen School of security studies, and applies both to post-communist Romania.

## Research question

> To what extent does violent crime represent a threat to societal security, and through which main mechanisms does it influence social stability?

## Approach

A qualitative, theoretical-explanatory study based on three complementary methods:

1. **Critical literature review** across criminology, the sociology of deviance and security studies, in both international and Romanian scholarship
2. **Analysis of official documents**, including Ministry of Internal Affairs reports, national statistics and crime-prevention strategies
3. **Interpretation of secondary statistical data** from Eurostat, the National Institute of Statistics (INS), the Ministry of Internal Affairs (MAI) and the EU Fundamental Rights Agency (FRA)

The thesis states an important limitation openly: official data reflect *recorded* crime, not *actual* crime, so a significant "dark figure" remains, especially for domestic violence.

## Theoretical framework

| Perspective | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Copenhagen School** (Buzan, Wæver, de Wilde): societal security and securitization theory | Treating violent crime as a potential threat to collective identity, cohesion and stability |
| **Classical criminological theories**: Merton's anomie theory, Hirschi's social control theory, Shaw and McKay's social disorganization theory | Explaining the causes of violent crime |
| **David Garland, *The Culture of Control*** | Understanding the institutional response and the tension between punitive and preventive logics |

## Structure

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Literature review and methodology | Three thematic axes: criminological, security studies and Romanian literature. Two gaps identified: security studies rarely treat violent crime as a societal threat, and Romanian scholarship has not linked criminology with security studies |
| **Chapter I** | Conceptual framework: violent crime and its typologies, societal security, and the analytical framework |
| **Chapter II** | Seven factors that generate violent crime: socio-economic conditions, family and socialization, social and cultural environment, social control, psychological factors, education and social exclusion, and alcohol and substance use |
| **Chapter III** | Romania as a case study: post-communist context, statistical trends (1990-2023), dominant types of violent offences, application of the analytical framework, a recent case of fatal intrafamily violence from June 2026, and the institutional response |
| Conclusions | Synthesis, findings for Romania, recommendations and limitations |

## Key findings

- **Violent crime is a real, documented and multidimensional threat to societal security.** It acts through both objective and subjective channels.
- **It operates through three mechanisms:** erosion of social cohesion and community social capital; reduced perceived legitimacy and capacity of institutions; and a diffuse climate of insecurity, amplified by media-driven securitization, that can exceed the objective scale of the phenomenon.
- **Causes are multifactorial.** The seven factors appear in different combinations across the main types of violent crime in Romania, at macro-structural, community and individual levels.
- **Romania's profile is distinctive.** Violent crime peaked during the post-communist transition, stabilized from the 2000s and has since diverged by offence type: classic violent offences kept declining while reported domestic violence increased. Domestic violence dominates the profile, and under-reporting is significant.
- **The institutional response is mostly reactive.** The thesis identifies three structural weaknesses: institutional fragmentation, insufficient resources, and the absence of an early-intervention system for documented family risk.

## Recommendations

An integrated approach that combines the repressive response with:

- stronger primary prevention
- better victim protection
- early intervention in at-risk families
- accessible mental health services for young people
- measures against early school leaving

## Limitations and future research

- Official statistics capture recorded crime only
- The case study relies solely on publicly available information; access to the full case file could refine the conclusions
- A comparative analysis with similar regional states (such as Bulgaria, Serbia or Hungary) would show whether the vulnerabilities are national or regional
